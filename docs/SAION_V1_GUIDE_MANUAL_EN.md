## 1. Welcome to SAION

I built SAION for people who want more control over how AI music sounds and feels.

Most AI tools understand lyrics and basic prompts. SAION adds the performance layer. That means you can guide emotion, pacing, breath, intensity, and delivery, not just the words.

SAION is not an AI music generator by itself. It is a performance notation system and creative control surface that helps AI models perform with more intent.

## 2. Why SAION Exists

Writers and artists often face the same problem: the words are right, but the performance is wrong.

Traditional lyric prompts can miss important details like:

1. Where to breathe
2. Which word to lean into
3. Where to pull back softly
4. How to shape emotion over a phrase

I built SAION to solve that gap.

Simple idea:

Lyrics -> SAION performance layer -> AI prompt -> generated result

## 3. The Performance Philosophy

I designed SAION around creative control, not one-click randomness.

My core principles:

1. You stay in charge.
2. Small changes should create clear, hearable differences.
3. Emotion and timing matter as much as lyrics.
4. Compare versions before choosing a final take.

The SAION workflow works best when it is iterative:

1. Create a base version
2. Adjust one or two things
3. Generate again
4. Compare
5. Keep the stronger result

## 5. Emotion Engine

I use the Emotion Engine to control the emotional center of a performance.

In plain terms, it answers:

1. How intense should this feel?
2. How open or vulnerable should the delivery be?
3. Should the tone feel confident or fragile?
4. Should the line feel relaxed or tense?

Best practice:

1. Start with a preset emotion profile.
2. Make one adjustment at a time.
3. Listen for emotional clarity, not loudness.

## 6. Vocal Performance Controls

These controls shape how the voice behaves in practice.

Common areas:

1. Delivery strength
2. Texture (smooth, airy, raspy, warm)
3. Timing feel (tight vs laid-back)
4. Breath presence
5. Runs and ornamentation

Practical workflow:

1. Pick a vocal preset close to your goal.
2. Use small dial changes for precision.
3. Re-generate and compare with your previous take.

## 7. Performance Notation Language

I treat SAION notation as a lightweight language for performance intent.

It helps communicate things words alone cannot.

### Common Symbols

1. ^ for lift or gentle emphasis
2. {b} for breath
3. ~ for sustain or trailing feel
4. > for crescendo or push
5. < for decrescendo or pull back
6. ! for accent
7. >> for forward push/compression
8. ALL CAPS for strong emphasis

Use notation sparingly and intentionally. Too much notation can make results inconsistent.

## 8. Performance Timeline

I break SAION into three simple phases:

1. Before generation
Set mood, vocal profile, prompt, and notation.

2. During generation
SAION compiles controls and text into machine-readable instructions.

3. After generation
Listen, compare, adjust, and regenerate.

If your output is close but not perfect, refine in small steps instead of rewriting everything.

## 9. AI Generation Workflow

This is the workflow I recommend for regular users:

1. Name your session
2. Enter a clear prompt
3. Set emotion and vocal direction
4. Add light notation if needed
5. Generate
6. Save as Version A
7. Make one controlled change
8. Generate again
9. Save as Version B
10. Compare A and B
11. Keep the better result and continue

This keeps decisions clean and avoids creative drift.

## 10. Performance Examples

### Example 1: Intimate Verse

Prompt idea:
Soft late-night vocal, close and personal, steady emotion.

Notation idea:
Use {b} at phrase breaks and a light ~ at line endings.

Expected result:
More breath detail, softer edges, smoother phrase endings.

### Example 2: Strong Chorus Lift

Prompt idea:
Emotional chorus with higher energy and confident delivery.

Notation idea:
Use > on key words and occasional ALL CAPS for emphasis.

Expected result:
More forward projection and clearer emotional peaks.

### Example 3: Controlled Contrast

Prompt idea:
Verse restrained, chorus open and bold.

Notation idea:
Use < in verse and > in chorus to shape contrast.

Expected result:
Sharper emotional transition between sections.

## 11. Notation Reference

Use this quick reference while writing or editing performance text.

| Symbol | Meaning | Example | AI Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ^ | Lift or gentle emphasis | ^hold^ this line | Slightly elevate delivery |
| {b} | Breath marker | stay {b} with me | Insert audible or implied breath |
| ~ | Sustain or trailing tone | forever~ | Extend ending tone |
| > | Crescendo or push | >rise now | Increase energy/drive |
| < | Decrescendo or soften | <easy now | Pull back intensity |
| ! | Accent | now! | Add attack/emphasis |
| >> | Push forward | >>move ahead | Tighten and drive phrase |
| ALL CAPS | Strong emphasis | NEVER let go | Increase stress/projection |

Note: Keep symbols meaningful. Overuse can blur performance intent.

## 12. Technical Architecture (Simple View)

You do not need to be technical to use SAION. This simple view explains what happens when I hit Generate.

1. Frontend: where you set prompts and controls
2. SAION layer: combines your choices into structured instructions
3. Provider layer: sends instructions to the selected AI model
4. Result layer: returns audio for playback and comparison

In short: you design the intent, SAION translates it, and the AI renders it.

## 13. Advanced Workflows

When you are ready for deeper control, this is how I suggest working:

3. Keep a version history by section (Verse, Pre, Chorus)
4. Test one variable per generation pass
5. Use A/B checkpoints to prevent losing strong takes

Advanced does not mean complicated. It means more deliberate.
